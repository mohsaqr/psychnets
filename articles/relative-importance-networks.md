# Relative-importance networks with the psychnets R package

How much of a model’s explanatory power can be attributed to each
predictor when the predictors overlap? In psychological and educational
questionnaires, self-efficacy, motivation, learning strategies and
self-regulation may explain some of the same variation in an outcome.
Multiple regression coefficients describe each predictor’s adjusted
association, but do not allocate the variance explained jointly by
several predictors. Relative importance analysis instead partitions the
model’s $`R^2`$ among predictors, including their shared contributions.
Approaches to this problem include dominance analysis (Budescu, 1993)
and other relative importance indices (Johnson & LeBreton, 2004). The
LMG decomposition (Lindeman et al., 1980; Groemping, 2006) calculates
each predictor’s average increase in $`R^2`$ over all possible orders in
which predictors can enter the model.

Psychological networks offer a complementary way to study relationships
among measured constructs. Rather than designating a single outcome in
advance, a network represents variables as nodes and their relationships
as edges. In education, network approaches have been used to examine
complex learning processes and the relations among psychological
constructs (Saqr et al., 2024). Studies of self-regulated learning have
investigated the dynamics of regulation during academic writing (Saqr et
al., 2021) and the association between students’ monitoring events and
phases of regulation (Malmberg et al., 2022). These applications
illustrate the value of examining a system of related learning processes
rather than interpreting each construct in isolation. However, an edge
in a conventional partial correlation network represents a conditional
association, not a share of explained variance.

A relative-importance network combines the network representation with
an explained-variance decomposition. Each construct is treated in turn
as the outcome of a regression on all the other constructs. The LMG
shares become directed edges: an edge from A to B gives the proportion
of B’s total variance attributed to A within that regression. The
incoming edges sum to B’s $`R^2`$, whereas outgoing edges summarise how
much A contributes across the other outcomes. This direction indicates
which variable is the predictor and which is the outcome; it does not
imply a causal effect. Relative-importance networks are therefore useful
when the research aim is to compare explanatory contributions among
correlated constructs, rather than to estimate direct conditional
associations or causal pathways.

This article demonstrates the approach with five self-regulated learning
constructs using the `psychnets` package. It introduces the LMG
decomposition, fits and interprets a directed network, verifies that the
incoming shares sum to each construct’s explained variance, and
distinguishes predictability from outgoing contribution. It then
compares Pearson-based results with a network based on Spearman rank
correlations and outlines reporting and interpretation considerations.
The example uses language-model-generated questionnaire responses, so
its numerical findings illustrate the method rather than provide
evidence about students.

``` r

library(psychnets)
```

## The model

For each outcome $`j`$, the other nodes form the predictor set $`P`$.
The LMG share of predictor $`k`$ is its average incremental contribution
to $`R^2`$ over all possible orders of predictor entry. Equivalently, it
averages the change in $`R^2`$ when $`k`$ is added to a subset $`S`$ of
the remaining predictors, with weights that give each ordering equal
importance:

``` math
\varphi_{kj} = \sum_{S \subseteq P \setminus \{k\}}
\frac{|S|!\,(|P| - |S| - 1)!}{|P|!}
\left[R^2(S \cup \{k\}) - R^2(S)\right].
```

Each $`R^2(A)`$ follows from the correlation matrix $`\mathbf{S}`$ as
$`R^2(A) = \mathbf{S}_{jA}\mathbf{S}_{AA}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{Aj}`$. The
shares are non-negative and add up to the outcome’s full-model explained
variance,

``` math
\sum_{k} \varphi_{kj} = R_j^2 .
```

The edge from $`k`$ to $`j`$ has weight $`\varphi_{kj}`$. Its value is a
share of the outcome’s total variance, not a fraction of the explained
variance alone. Incoming weights sum to the outcome’s $`R^2`$, while
outgoing weights sum the predictor’s contributions across the other
outcomes. These totals have different interpretations and should not be
compared as though they measured the same property. For $`p`$ nodes,
each outcome involves $`2^{p-1}`$ predictor subsets. Computation
therefore becomes increasingly demanding as the network grows, and the
default limit is 21 nodes.

## Data

`SRL_GPT` contains 300 sets of responses to the Motivated Strategies for
Learning Questionnaire, represented by five construct scores on scales
from 1 to 7. These responses were generated by a large language model
(GPT) for a study of how language models answer psychological surveys
(Vogelsmeier et al., 2025). They provide an illustrative dataset with
related constructs, not observations of students. Accordingly, the
results below should not be interpreted as findings about student
behaviour or learning.

- **Nodes:** five construct scores, treated as continuous: `CSU`
  (cognitive strategy use), `IV` (intrinsic value), `SE`
  (self-efficacy), `SR` (self-regulation) and `TA` (test anxiety). Each
  is in turn the outcome of a linear regression and a predictor in the
  four others.
- **Observations:** one row per respondent; the data are complete.

## How is each construct’s explained variance divided?

[`psychnet()`](https://pak.dynasite.org/psychnets/reference/psychnet.md)
with `method = "relimp"` estimates a relative-importance network by
treating each of the five constructs in turn as an outcome and
decomposing its regression $`R^2`$ among the other four constructs.

``` r

importance_net <- psychnet(data = SRL_GPT, method = "relimp")
summary(importance_net)
#> <psychnet> relimp network
#>   nodes: 5   edges: 20   (directed)
#>   structural certificate (decomposition residual): 2.22e-16
#>   edge weight: range [0.006, 0.299], mean 0.165
```

The fitted network contains 20 directed edges, one for each ordered pair
of distinct constructs. All estimated shares are positive, ranging from
0.006 to 0.299 when rounded to three decimal places. In the edge table,
`from` identifies the predictor and `to` identifies the outcome:

``` r

as.data.frame(importance_net)

#> 3    SR CSU 0.274082666
#> 4    TA CSU 0.007326148
#> 5   CSU  IV 0.299473895

#> 7    SR  IV 0.258608009
```

The largest edge runs from cognitive strategy use to intrinsic value
(0.299). This means that cognitive strategy use is allocated
approximately 29.9% of the total variance in intrinsic value, within the
regression containing all four other constructs. The reverse edge, from
intrinsic value to cognitive strategy use, has weight 0.289. Although
these values are similar, they refer to different outcomes and need not
be equal. Among cognitive strategy use, intrinsic value, self-efficacy
and self-regulation, the directed shares range from 0.213 to 0.299. All
three other learning constructs therefore make appreciable contributions
to each of these outcomes.

The pattern differs for test anxiety. Self-regulation has an incoming
share of 0.128 for test anxiety, compared with 0.014 for cognitive
strategy use, 0.015 for intrinsic value and 0.012 for self-efficacy. In
the reverse direction, test anxiety contributes between 0.006 and 0.007
to the explained variance of cognitive strategy use, intrinsic value and
self-efficacy, and 0.052 to that of self-regulation. Thus,
self-regulation has the largest relative-importance link with test
anxiety in both directions. These shares do not establish that
self-regulation mediates the relations between test anxiety and the
other learning constructs.

## Do the shares add up to each construct’s explained variance?

An important check is whether the incoming shares reproduce the $`R^2`$
of each full regression.
[`certificate()`](https://pak.dynasite.org/psychnets/reference/certificate.md)
reports the largest absolute difference between these quantities across
the five nodes. A value near zero indicates that the estimated weights
satisfy the decomposition identity to numerical precision.

``` r

certificate(importance_net)
#>   method  certificate       kind certified
#> 1 relimp 2.220446e-16 structural      TRUE
```

The reported discrepancy is $`2.220446 \times 10^{-16}`$, below
$`10^{-15}`$ and consistent with numerical rounding. The edge weights
thus reproduce each outcome’s explained variance to machine precision.
This is a check of the decomposition, not evidence that the regressions
meet their assumptions or fit the data adequately.

## How much of each construct is explained?

[`net_predict()`](https://pak.dynasite.org/psychnets/reference/net_predict.md)
gives the $`R^2`$ for each construct when predicted by all four others.
This is also the sum of that construct’s incoming raw
relative-importance shares.

``` r

net_predict(importance_net)
#>   node     type metric predictability accuracy
#> 1  CSU gaussian     R2      0.8334882       NA
#> 2   IV gaussian     R2      0.7814012       NA
#> 3   SE gaussian     R2      0.7262171       NA
#> 4   SR gaussian     R2      0.7956941       NA
#> 5   TA gaussian     R2      0.1693997       NA
```

The four predictors jointly account for approximately 83% of the
variance in cognitive strategy use ($`R^2 = 0.833`$), 80% in
self-regulation ($`R^2 = 0.796`$), 78% in intrinsic value
($`R^2 = 0.781`$) and 73% in self-efficacy ($`R^2 = 0.726`$).
Predictability is substantially lower for test anxiety
($`R^2 = 0.169`$): the other constructs explain approximately 17% of its
variance. Self-regulation’s share of 0.128 represents about three
quarters of this explained variance, with most of test anxiety’s total
variance remaining unexplained by the model.

## Which constructs contribute most to explaining the others?

[`net_centralities()`](https://pak.dynasite.org/psychnets/reference/net_centralities.md)
reports each node’s outgoing strength, the sum of its contributions to
the regressions for the other constructs. Since every edge weight is
non-negative, strength and expected influence are identical for this
network.

``` r

net_centralities(importance_net)
#>   node  strength expected_influence
#> 1  CSU 0.8716118          0.8716118
#> 2   IV 0.7759844          0.7759844
#> 3   SE 0.7047411          0.7047411
#> 4   SR 0.8810358          0.8810358
#> 5   TA 0.0728272          0.0728272
```

Self-regulation has the highest outgoing strength (0.881), followed
closely by cognitive strategy use (0.872). Test anxiety has the lowest
(0.073). Self-regulation’s leading total reflects its contributions to
the other learning constructs as well as its comparatively large share
in test anxiety. Outgoing strength summarises how much a construct
contributes to explaining the other outcomes across separate
regressions. It should be reported separately from incoming $`R^2`$,
which describes how well the construct itself is explained.

## What does the network look like?

[`cograph::splot()`](https://sonsoles.me/cograph/reference/splot.html)
displays the network with arrows indicating the predictor-to-outcome
direction and edge labels showing the raw LMG shares.

``` r

cograph::splot(importance_net, directed = TRUE, edge_labels = TRUE)
```

![](relative-importance-networks_files/figure-html/plot-1.png)

Because the network contains all 20 possible directed edges, the plot is
relatively dense. The larger reciprocal shares among the four learning
constructs contrast with the generally smaller links involving test
anxiety. The edge table is preferable when precise numerical comparisons
are needed.

## Do the conclusions depend on the correlation estimator?

rank-based associations for Pearson correlations in the decomposition;
it does not change the variables or the number of nodes.

The largest directed share is again from cognitive strategy use to
intrinsic value (0.299). For the four learning constructs, rank-based
$`R^2`$ values range from 0.712 to 0.828, compared with 0.726 to 0.833
in the Pearson-based network. Test anxiety shows a more noticeable
difference: its $`R^2`$ decreases from 0.169 to 0.116, while
self-regulation’s share in that outcome decreases from 0.128 to 0.086.
The broad pattern of larger shares among the learning constructs is
similar under both estimators, but the strength of the
explained-variance links involving test anxiety is sensitive to the
correlation method.

## Reporting the analysis

When reporting a relative-importance network, researchers should specify
the constructs, sample size, correlation estimator, handling of missing
values, and whether the displayed edge weights are raw or normalised.
Edge directions should be defined as predictor to outcome, with weights
interpreted on the appropriate variance scale. Incoming $`R^2`$ and
outgoing strength answer distinct questions and should be presented
separately. Sensitivity analyses can help readers assess how strongly
conclusions depend on the chosen correlation estimator.

For example, this analysis used raw LMG shares based on Pearson
correlations from 300 complete sets of language-model-generated
responses. The network contained 20 directed edges, of which the largest
connected cognitive strategy use to intrinsic value (0.299). Node
predictability ranged from $`R^2 = 0.169`$ for test anxiety to
$`R^2 = 0.833`$ for cognitive strategy use. Self-regulation accounted
for 0.128 of test anxiety’s total variance, the largest incoming share
for that outcome. Under Spearman correlations, the learning constructs
retained broadly similar patterns, whereas test anxiety’s $`R^2`$ fell
to 0.116.

## Assumptions and when to use relative importance

- Each outcome is modelled through a regression on the remaining nodes.
  Interpretation therefore depends on the suitability of the regression
  and correlation assumptions, including linear relationships and
  independence of observations.
- Relative importance partitions explained variance among correlated
  predictors. It does not estimate unique conditional associations. When
  those associations are the research focus, a partial correlation
  network, such as one estimated with the graphical lasso, is more
  suitable.
- Raw weights retain the outcome’s variance scale: a share of 0.1
  represents one tenth of its total variance. `normalized = TRUE`
  instead rescales each outcome’s incoming shares to sum to one. Such
  weights compare the relative allocation within an outcome but no
  longer show how much variance the predictors collectively explain.
- Edge direction identifies the predictor and outcome in a regression.
  It does not establish temporal order, causal influence or mediation.
- The number of predictor subsets doubles with each additional node.
  This makes the method most practical for small or moderate networks.
